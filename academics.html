How We Teach

Two Strands, One Timetable

Lessons at Raayat follow Uganda’s Competency Based Curriculum (CBC), the national syllabus set by the National Curriculum Development Centre. It asks pupils to do rather than only to memorise — to measure, build, discuss, write and explain.

Alongside it runs our theology strand: Qur’an recitation, Arabic and Islamic Studies. These are timetabled subjects with their own teachers and their own assessment, not an after-school extra.

The two strands share one school day, and a child’s report card carries both.

Baby · Middle · Top

Nursery Section

The nursery years are about settling into school happily and building the foundations for Primary One. Children spend their day moving between play, song, story and short structured activities.

What children learn

  • Letter sounds, handwriting patterns and early reading
  • Counting, sorting, shapes, colours and simple number work
  • Speaking and listening in English
  • Short surahs, daily du’as and the manners that go with them
  • Sharing, tidying up, toileting and other habits of independence

Primary One – Three

Lower Primary

In lower primary, reading, writing and number work turn into solid, usable skills. Lessons are activity based and pupils are assessed continuously rather than only at the end of term.

Subjects

  • Literacy and English
  • Numeracy / Mathematics
  • Religious Education and Islamic Studies
  • Qur’an recitation and Arabic
  • News, creative activities and physical education
Primary Four – Seven

Upper Primary

From Primary Four the four examinable subjects take centre stage, and preparation for the Primary Leaving Examination (PLE) builds steadily through P.6 and P.7 with revision, past papers and close follow-up of every candidate.

Subjects

  • English Language
  • Mathematics
  • Integrated Science
  • Social Studies and Religious Education
  • Islamic Studies, Qur’an and Arabic

Candidates also receive guidance on choosing a secondary school and completing their selection forms.

Keeping Track

Assessment & Reporting

How we measure progress, and how you hear about it.

01

Continuous Assessment

CBC work is assessed as it happens — through classwork, projects and practical tasks, not only written tests.

02

End of Term Reports

Every pupil receives a report covering both academic subjects and the theology strand, plus conduct and attendance.

03

Parents’ Days

Meet your child’s class teacher face to face, collect the report and agree what to work on next term.

04

Early Contact

If something changes — attendance, behaviour or a sudden drop in work — the office calls you before the term ends.
